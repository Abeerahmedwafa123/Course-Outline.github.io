Business Result Starter – Course Outline & Syllabus

Complete overview, unit-by-unit syllabus, and a 2-sessions-per-week teaching plan.

Course Description

This course is based on Business Result Second Edition – Starter. It introduces learners to the essential communication skills they need in modern international workplaces. The syllabus is communication-driven and focuses on real business tasks: introductions, talking about work and companies, handling simple phone calls and emails, and describing products, departments, technology, and travel.

Course Duration & Structure

8 Units Approx. 32 contact hours 2 sessions per week ~16 weeks total

Each unit provides around four hours of classroom material, plus additional practice through Practice Files, Online Practice, and revision tasks.

Learning Objectives

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

  • Introduce themselves and colleagues professionally.
  • Talk about companies, departments, workplaces, and products.
  • Use basic grammar accurately (forms of to be, simple present, Wh- questions).
  • Spell names, say numbers, and give phone, email, and website information.
  • Handle basic phone calls and write simple business emails.
  • Participate in short, realistic business conversations and role-plays.

Unit 1 – You

Focus

  • Introducing yourself.
  • Talking about jobs and job titles.
  • Asking about names and jobs.
  • Alphabet and spelling names.
  • Meeting people in business contexts.

Language

  • Grammar: to be (I / you) – affirmative, negative, questions.
  • Business communication: greetings, first meetings, introductions, goodbyes.

Unit 2 – Company

Focus

  • Talking about companies and countries.
  • Head offices, locations, where people work.
  • Saying numbers 0–9.
  • Starting and answering a phone call.

Language

  • Grammar: to be (he / she / it) – affirmative, negative, questions.
  • Business communication: answering the phone, asking for someone, polite requests.

Unit 3 – Workplace

Focus

  • Describing your company and workplace.
  • Vocabulary: reception, office, warehouse, factory, cafeteria, car park.
  • Adjectives: big, small, old, new, good, etc.
  • Email and website addresses.
  • Emailing a simple request.

Language

  • Grammar: to be (we / they) + Wh- questions (What / Where / Who).
  • Business communication: structure of simple business emails, basic phrases.

Unit 4 – Departments

Focus

  • Company departments and internal structure.
  • Daily tasks and responsibilities.

Language

  • Grammar: Present simple for routines (basic forms).
  • Business communication: talking about job roles, asking about responsibilities.

Viewpoint 1 – People in Business

Video-based lesson linked to Units 1–4: authentic interviews and case studies about people in business. Focus on listening, note-taking, and discussion.

Unit 5 – Products

Focus

  • Describing products, features, and quality.
  • Talking about size, function, and type.

Language

  • Grammar: this / that / these / those; basic countable/uncountable use.
  • Business communication: asking for and giving product information.

Unit 6 – Entertaining

Focus

  • Inviting and entertaining clients.
  • Talking about food, restaurants, and social activities.
  • Accepting and declining invitations.

Language

  • Grammar (functional): modal can for polite requests and offers.
  • Business communication: arranging social events, polite responses.

Unit 7 – Technology

Focus

  • Office technology and devices.
  • Talking about technical problems.
  • Giving simple instructions.

Language

  • Grammar: have / has; imperatives for instructions.
  • Business communication: reporting a problem, explaining what to do.

Unit 8 – Travel

Focus

  • Travel arrangements and timetables.
  • Flight numbers, times, and prices.
  • Booking and confirming travel.

Language

  • Grammar: present simple for timetables; time expressions.
  • Business communication: asking for and giving travel information.

Viewpoint 2 – Describing Businesses

Video-based lesson linked to Units 5–8. Students watch case studies and practise describing companies, products, and services.

Structure

This plan assumes 2 sessions per week, each around 90 minutes. Each unit takes about 4 sessions, including practice and revision.

Week 1 – Unit 1 (Part 1)

Session 1

  • Starting Point: names and basic introductions.
  • Working with Words: jobs & job titles.
  • Pronunciation: word stress in job titles.

Session 2

  • Language at Work: to be (I/you) – forms & questions.
  • Practically Speaking: alphabet and spelling names.
  • Business Communication: greetings, meeting people.

Week 2 – Unit 1 (Part 2) & Unit 2 Start

Session 3

  • Unit 1: conversation practice using names & jobs.
  • Talking Point: conference role-play (introductions).

Session 4

  • Unit 2 Working with Words: companies & countries, head office.
  • Listening: business cards & company information.

Week 3 – Unit 2 (Part 2)

Session 5

  • Language at Work: to be (he/she/it).
  • Practically Speaking: numbers 0–9, phone digits.
  • Listening: phone numbers and passcodes.

Session 6

  • Business Communication: starting phone calls, asking for people.
  • Talking Point: Company game (recycling Unit 2 language).

Week 4 – Unit 3 (Part 1)

Session 7

  • Working with Words: workplace locations (office, reception, factory, etc.).
  • Adjectives: old, new, big, small, good.

Session 8

  • Language at Work: to be (we/they), Wh- questions (What/Where/Who).
  • Practice: describing workplaces and people.

Week 5 – Unit 3 (Part 2)

Session 9

  • Practically Speaking: email and website addresses.
  • Listening: spelling and repeating details.

Session 10

  • Business Communication: writing simple request emails.
  • Talking Point: question-and-answer practice about workplace.
  • Short review of Units 1–3.

Week 6 – Unit 4 (Part 1)

Session 11

  • Working with Words: departments and company structure.
  • Vocabulary: HR, Finance, IT, etc.

Session 12

  • Language at Work: present simple (basic routines).
  • Speaking: talking about departmental tasks.

Week 7 – Unit 4 (Part 2) & Viewpoint 1

Session 13

  • Business Communication: describing job roles & responsibilities.
  • Role-plays: Who does what?

Session 14

  • Viewpoint 1 – People in Business video lesson.
  • Listening, note-taking, and discussion activities.

Week 8 – Unit 5 (Part 1)

Session 15

  • Working with Words: products and features.
  • Grammar: this / that / these / those.

Session 16

  • Practice: describing simple products.
  • Controlled dialogues using demonstratives.

Week 9 – Unit 5 (Part 2) & Unit 6 Start

Session 17

  • Business Communication: asking for and giving product information.
  • Speaking: short product presentations.

Session 18

  • Unit 6 Working with Words: food, restaurants, and social activities.
  • Invitations vocabulary.

Week 10 – Unit 6 (Part 2)

Session 19

  • Business Communication: inviting, accepting, declining.
  • Role-plays: client dinners, social events.

Session 20

  • Arranging meetings and social plans.
  • Mini review of Units 4–6.

Week 11 – Unit 7 (Part 1)

Session 21

  • Working with Words: office technology and devices.

Session 22

  • Language at Work: have / has; imperatives (instructions).
  • Practising basic technical instructions.

Week 12 – Unit 7 (Part 2) & Unit 8 Start

Session 23

  • Business Communication: reporting technical problems & giving solutions.
  • Technology role-plays.

Session 24

  • Unit 8 Working with Words: travel vocabulary (tickets, flights, bookings).

Week 13 – Unit 8 (Part 2)

Session 25

  • Language at Work: present simple for timetables, time expressions.
  • Practise reading schedules.

Session 26

  • Business Communication: asking for/giving travel information.
  • Dialogue practice for booking and confirming travel.

Week 14 – Unit 8 (Consolidation) & Viewpoint 2

Session 27

  • Talking Point: travel arrangements task.
  • Fluency practice linking all travel language.

Session 28

  • Viewpoint 2 – Describing Businesses video lesson.
  • Discussion and speaking practice.

Week 15 – Global Review

Session 29

  • Global review of Units 1–8 (vocabulary and grammar).
  • Practice activities from Practice Files / revision games.

Session 30

  • Speaking test practice: role-plays, presentations, phone calls.

Week 16 – Assessment & Feedback

Session 31

  • Final review and Q&A.
  • Individual speaking performances (short tasks).

Session 32

  • Final Progress Test (written + short speaking assessment).
  • Course feedback and next steps.

You can easily adjust session lengths or merge sessions depending on your institutional timetable.
